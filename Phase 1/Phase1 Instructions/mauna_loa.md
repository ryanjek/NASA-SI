# Mauna Loa and HI-SEAS

The habitat and the lava are different records. HI-SEAS is an operations facility. The searchable analog is the young basaltic surface around it, and only for the processes that were actually sampled.

## Habitat

Wikipedia prints the habitat at 19.602378°N, 155.487192°W, about 8200 ft (2500 m), on the north slope of Mauna Loa. Bleacher et al. (2017) say about 2400 m. The point uses the Wikipedia coordinate and records both elevations. The habitat is a former quarry on a spatter cone. Crew isolation, selection, and communications delay are not ground features, so the site is not a search seed.

A habitat-website comparison between local weathered basalt and Curiosity's CheMin mineralogy was not used. The mineralogy claim below comes from the opened alteration abstract.

## Lava tubes

Bleacher et al. (2017) studied two tube segments on the north flank with the HI-SEAS and RIS⁴E teams. The abstract does not print coordinates, so the tubes are unlocated. The flows are described as about 200 years old and sparsely vegetated. They argue that young Earth basalt is a better alteration-state match to older Martian basalt because aqueous alteration is faster on Earth. Neighboring Mauna Loa flows emplaced less than 1 km away are given as 1880–1940, and the site receives acidic aerosol from Kīlauea while sitting high enough to limit sea salt.

The tubes were entered through skylights. Surface magnetics and 270 MHz GPR detected void space. Interior lidar mapped the tube. The abstract is explicit that surface geophysics confirmed a void and did not by itself recover the tube dimensions. Coatings include Na sulfates, confirmed by XRD, plus calcite, gypsum, and monohydrocalcite. Preliminary sequencing of those deposits found Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria. Those minerals and communities are inside the tube. A surface roughness layer is not a substitute.

## Pāhoehoe alteration

Weert et al. (2021) sampled the top 10 cm of horizontal pāhoehoe within 6 km of the habitat during EMMIHS-I. The catalog stores that radius around the habitat point. It is not a mapped flow outline. On flows older than about 400 years, weathering indices show alteration in the top 2–3 cm. Products are Fe/Mg-smectite, hydrated silica, Al-silicates, Al-hydroxide, and palagonite, formed with meteoric water in an arid to semi-arid, cool to moderate setting. They compare weathering indices with MER APXS data and report a similarly low degree of alteration. This is a conference abstract. It is not a CheMin result, and it does not name individual flow units. Flow names used later have to come from the USGS geologic map of Hawaii, which is cataloged and was not interpreted here.

The age split should stay visible: the tube study describes flows about 200 years old, and the alteration study restricts the rind development to pāhoehoe older than about 400 years. Those are different surfaces inside the same 6 km.

## What a later search can use

Summer NDVI and bare fraction can screen for the sparse lava. Slope and ruggedness can screen for rough flow surfaces, with the warning that a 30 m DEM misses ʻaʻā texture. EMIT, between about 52°N and 52°S, can be asked for clay, sulfate, and iron on Mauna Loa and must be left empty where it has no valid pixels. EMIT does not sample a tube interior or a 2–3 cm depth profile. GLiM can mark the island as volcanic. It cannot separate pāhoehoe from ʻaʻā.
