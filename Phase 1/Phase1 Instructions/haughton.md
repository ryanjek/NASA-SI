# Haughton impact structure

Haughton is a polar-desert impact crater in carbonate and evaporite rock. It is a process analog for impact hydrothermal systems, icy breccia, and field operations. It is not a basalt lithology twin of Mars, and a later search should keep it from matching Mauna Loa or Askja on rock type.

## Center, size, and age

Patterson and Ernst (2020) place the center at 75°22′38″N, 89°40′48″W. The Earth Impact Database page for Haughton did not load during this pass, so that database was not used for the coordinate. Osinski et al. (2005a) give an apparent diameter of about 23 km, a final rim of about 16 km, and a transient cavity of about 10–12 km, in about 1880 m of Paleozoic carbonates with evaporites over crystalline basement. The crater-fill melt groundmass includes calcite, silicate glass, and anhydrite. There is no central topographic peak in that interpretation.

The age is contested. Sherlock et al. (2005) report about 39 Ma from Ar/Ar on shocked basement clasts and say older Miocene ages are difficult to reconcile with that result. Young et al. (2013) report a zircon (U-Th)/He age of 23.5 ± 2.0 Ma, close to the older fission-track and Ar/Ar ages near 23 Ma. Osinski et al. (2005a) use about 39 Ma. The Haughton-Mars Project page says about 23 million years and 20 km. The catalog does not collapse those figures. The website diameter is not the mapped apparent diameter.

## Four hydrothermal settings

Osinski et al. (2005b) describe a moderate- to low-temperature system driven by groundwater in hot impact melt breccia. The four settings, all unlocated as sample points, are:

- Vugs and veins in the crater-fill melt breccia, stronger toward the base.
- Cementation inside the central uplift, including two quartz episodes.
- Intense veining on the faulted outer margin of the central uplift.
- Pipes or gossans and mineralization on rim faults.

Reported minerals include quartz, calcite, marcasite, pyrite, celestite, barite, and fluorite. Early temperatures were at least about 200°C, and later precipitation continued below about 100°C. Hydrocarbons from the country rock moved during the hotter calcite stage. Distance to the crater only locates a candidate. It does not identify which setting, if any, is present. The geologic map is a paper figure, cataloged as a site map.

The Haughton Formation lake sediments overlie eroded impactites and uplifted target rocks. They are a later paleolake record, not the hydrothermal assemblage.

## Permafrost, Drill Hill, and habitats

Glass and Seitz (2021) place Drill Hill at 75°25.25′N, 89°45.60′W on fallback breccia about 150 m thick, with intermittent clear ice. The summer active layer on the carbonate-rich, ice-wedge polygon terrain is about 50–70 cm. They cite continuous permafrost of roughly 600 m to a paper that was not opened here, so that thickness stays a citation, not a checked measurement. Drill Hill is a physical analog for icy-breccia drilling and an operations record for prototype drills. The seasonal active layer makes it a different ground-ice regime from University Valley, where air temperature does not rise above freezing.

The opened FMARS manuscript places that habitat at 75°25′52.39″N, 89°49′28.05″W, 267.7 m, on the northwest rim. It describes simulated extravehicular sampling of shocked rock and points to a companion manuscript, then in press, on endolithic habitats. That companion paper was not opened, so this catalog supports the sampling context only. The Mars Institute page places the Haughton-Mars Project Research Station at 75°26′N, 89°52′W. That is a different operations point from FMARS. Neither habitat is a search seed.

The same project page compares Devon Island glacial troughs, including Astronaut Canyon, with a tributary of Ius Chasma. That is a shape-and-setting hypothesis from a project page. Valley shape can be measured later. The site is not a seed, because the comparison does not establish how the Martian canyons formed.

## What a later search can use

GLiM lithology is the feature that keeps this carbonate target from scoring as basalt. Impact distance is a locator for the Haughton process family only. Climate layers can show a polar desert with seasonal warmth, which is the active-layer regime, not dry permafrost. They cannot see hydrothermal minerals, breccia ice, or the unresolved impact age.
