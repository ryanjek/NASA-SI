# Askja and Dyngjufjöll

The Stimson analog is the basaltic sand sheet east-southeast of Askja, not the caldera, the 1875 rhyolitic pumice, or the Apollo training history. Claims and the missing coordinates are in `data/phase1/`.

## Caldera context

The opened Wikipedia page, citing the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, places Askja at 65.033°N, 16.783°W. The listed summit is 1516 m. The same page says the main crater floor is about 1100 m, so that summit is not the floor being compared with a sand sheet. The page also gives the rain shadow northeast of Vatnajökull as about 450 mm of rain a year. That is a cold desert in the Icelandic literature, and it is much wetter than the Dry Valleys. One aridity threshold should not be shared across those analogs.

Apollo crews trained on the geology here. That is historical context. It is not a physical feature.

## Dyngjusandur

Sara et al. (2017), using the facies in Mountney and Russell (2004), describe a sand sheet about 40 km long and 240 km2, from about 10 cm to about 10 m thick. No centroid was printed in the opened abstract, so the sheet and its facies are unlocated until a figure is digitized.

Three facies matter:

- Southwest: deflationary, very fine to medium basaltic sand with cobbles and boulders.
- Center: inflating, very fine sand, relict lava, and ripples. This is the part with the millimeter lamination and cross-bedding compared to Stimson.
- Northeast: accumulation limited to depressions, and sand dominated by 1875 Askja pumice plus local basalt. Sara et al. set this facies aside because the chemistry differs. It is a contrast, not a seed.

The 2014–2015 Holuhraun lava sits on the southeastern sheet. Sara et al. treat it as a later addition of ash and crystals that may shift MgO. It is a disturbance of the eolian deposit.

## Composition

Opened field results in Sara et al. (2017): the top centimeter is fine to medium sand of basalt fragments, glass, olivine, pyroxene, and plagioclase. Below that is a finer layer rich in angular glass. Trenches to about 1 m show millimeter laminations, some centimeter beds, and sand wedges. Bulk sand is reported at about 48–52 wt% SiO2, 5–8 wt% MgO, and 13–15 wt% Fe2O3. They compare the unaltered Stimson, a cross-bedded sandstone of plagioclase, pyroxene, magnetite, and amorphous material, with that structure and those oxide ranges.

This is a conference abstract. The facies framework is the Mountney and Russell paper; this pass opened that paper's landing page, not the full text. Oxide values are laboratory measurements. Askja is outside the EMIT latitude window, near 52°N to 52°S, so those values must not be filled from an EMIT map.

## What a later search can use

A DEM can separate the low-relief sheet from the caldera walls. Summer NDVI can screen for bare sand. Neither one identifies the northeast pumice facies or the grain mineralogy. The published facies figures are cataloged as a site map, not as a global layer.
